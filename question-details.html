DUI / DWI Answered

Can I refuse a breathalyzer in Florida without losing my license?

I was pulled over last night on I-4 for what the officer said was drifting between lanes. He asked me to do some roadside exercises and then to blow into a breathalyzer. I refused both. He arrested me for DUI anyway and I was released this morning.

I have never been in trouble before. Is my license automatically gone because I refused? Is there anything I should be doing right now, before I even hire someone?

Amir A. Ladan Criminal Defense & DUI · Orlando, FL · 15 years · 4.9 ★ (128)

First, take a breath. A refusal is a serious problem but it is not the end of the road, and you have more control right now than you probably feel.

Florida's implied consent law means that by holding a license you agreed to a breath, blood or urine test when lawfully arrested for DUI. A first refusal triggers a one-year administrative suspension, separate from anything that happens in criminal court. But that suspension is not final yet.

The single most important thing: you have 10 days from the arrest to request a formal review hearing with the DHSMV. If you do, you can usually get a temporary permit to keep driving while the hearing is pending, and the hearing itself is a chance to challenge whether the stop and arrest were lawful. Miss the 10 days and the suspension simply takes effect.

Your refusal of the roadside exercises is a different matter. Those are voluntary, and refusing them is far less damaging than people assume. Write down everything you remember about the stop today while it is fresh, and talk to a DUI attorney before that 10-day window closes.

Maria E. Gonzalez Criminal Defense · Orlando, FL · 4.7 ★ (72)

Agree with the answer above on the 10-day deadline, and I want to add one practical point. If this is truly a first refusal, you may be eligible to waive the formal review and apply immediately for a hardship license instead, which lets you drive to work and essential appointments without the risk of losing at the hearing.

Which route is better depends on how strong the officer's basis for the stop was. "Drifting between lanes" is one of the most contested reasons for a stop, so do not assume the case against you is solid.

John P. Morgan DUI Defense · Orlando, FL · 4.8 ★ (96)

One thing nobody has mentioned: request the arrest report and any dash or body camera footage as soon as possible. In refusal cases the prosecution leans heavily on the officer's description of your driving and demeanor, and the video very often tells a calmer story. Your attorney can request it, but knowing to ask early matters.

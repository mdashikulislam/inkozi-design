Can I refuse a breathalyzer in Florida without losing my license?

I was pulled over last night on I-4 for what the officer said was drifting between lanes. He asked me to do some roadside exercises and then to blow into the breathalyzer. I refused both. He took my license and handed me a paper.

I have never been in trouble before. Is my license automatically gone because I refused? Is there anything I should be doing right now?

    First, take a breath. A refusal is a serious problem but it is not the end of the road, and you have more control than you think. Florida's implied consent law means a refusal triggers a one-year administrative suspension, separate from criminal court. The single most important thing: you have 10 days from the arrest to request a formal review hearing with the DHSMV.

    Agree with the answer above on the 10-day deadline, and I want to add one practical point. If this is truly a first refusal you may be eligible to waive the hearing in exchange for immediate hardship-license eligibility. Which route is better depends on how strong the officer's basis for the stop was.

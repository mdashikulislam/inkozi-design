What to Do Immediately After a DUI Arrest in Florida

Amir A. Ladan
Criminal Defense Attorney
October 12, 2024
5 min read

Navigating the aftermath of a DUI arrest can be confusing and scary. The decisions you make in the first 24 to 48 hours are critical and can significantly impact the outcome of your case. Here are the essential steps you must take to protect your rights.

1. Remain Silent and Do Not Incriminate Yourself

The most important rule following an arrest is to exercise your right to remain silent. Law enforcement officers are trained to elicit information that can be used against you in court. While you must provide basic identifying information (like your name and driver's license), you are not obligated to answer questions about where you were going, what you were doing, or if you had been drinking.

"Anything you say can and will be used against you in a court of law. The best defense begins with knowing when to stop talking."

2. Request to Speak with an Attorney Immediately

As soon as you are arrested, clearly state that you wish to speak with an attorney. Do not agree to any further questioning or testing (aside from what is legally required by implied consent laws) until you have consulted with legal counsel. An experienced DUI lawyer will guide you on how to proceed without damaging your case.

Understanding Implied Consent in Florida

Florida law operates under "implied consent," meaning that by holding a driver's license, you have implicitly agreed to submit to approved chemical or physical tests (breath, blood, or urine) if lawfully arrested for DUI. Refusing these tests can result in an automatic suspension of your driver's license for a year or more, and the refusal can be used as evidence against you. However, you still have the right to refuse field sobriety exercises (like walking a straight line), which are highly subjective.

3. Document Everything You Remember

Once you are released, write down every detail you can remember about the traffic stop and the arrest. Key details include:

  • The exact reason the officer gave for pulling you over.
  • What the officer said to you and how they behaved.
  • The weather and road conditions at the time.
  • Whether you were read your Miranda rights.
  • Details about how the breathalyzer or field sobriety tests were administered.

4. Request a DMV Hearing

In Florida, you only have 10 days from the date of your arrest to request a formal review hearing with the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (DHSMV). If you fail to do this, your license will be automatically suspended. Your attorney can file this request on your behalf and represent you at the hearing, potentially allowing you to secure a hardship license so you can continue driving to work.

Conclusion

A DUI charge is serious, but an arrest is not a conviction. By staying calm, invoking your rights, and securing competent legal representation immediately, you can ensure your defense is as strong as possible from day one.
